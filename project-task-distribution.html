Three rules that keep it fair

The course grades revisions and cohesion, so these matter as much as the tasks.

You own your sections

Whatever you write in one artifact, you revise in the next one after feedback. Every task below ends with that revision step.

Mark every change

Removed text is struck through and new or changed text is highlighted yellow, all inside one master document.

Never wait on a teammate

Names, scales, criteria and templates are fixed below, so you write straight into your own section. The kickoff only creates the shared files. The review call two days before the deadline is where the work comes together.

Fixed before anyone starts

Everyone uses these exact names and numbers, so no one has to wait for another person's section to know what to write. Every item comes from the course brief or the Scenario 11 description.

Boundary rules

  • Screening lives in the Employer portal and uses match scores from the engine.
  • Course suggestions live in Career guidance and training. The engine only matches people to jobs.
  • The Analytics dashboard only reads platform data and never changes matches.
  • Security, accessibility, and Arabic and English support are constraints written into every story's notes. Admin work and government connections are stories in Integration and platform.

Methodology comparison

Plan-driven: Waterfall, Spiral. Agile: Scrum, Kanban. Each is scored 1 to 5 on six equally weighted criteria: requirement stability, stakeholder involvement, risk handling, fit with a mixed team, documentation for government oversight, and time to first usable release. The plan assumes Scrum wins.

Risk register workbook

Initial sheet: risk name, description, mitigation. Expanded sheet adds probability (0.1 to 0.9), impact (1 to 5), risk exposure (probability times impact), first indicator, and additional strategies.

Backlog and estimation

Story form: As a (role), I want (goal) so that (benefit). Fibonacci points 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13. Reference story: uploading a CV is 3 points. Two-week sprints, planned velocity of 30 points. Sheet columns: ID, epic, story, points, priority (MoSCoW), phase.

Metrics

  • Sprint velocity and burndown
  • Risk exposure trend
  • Defect escape rate
  • Match-to-application rate

Presentation storyline

The problem, then how it gets built, what could go wrong, and how everyone stays informed. Persona: a new graduate who applied on five job boards and heard nothing back. The shared deck uses a built-in theme.

Created together at each kickoff: the master document (one heading per artifact and the change-mark legend), the risk workbook, the backlog sheet, and the slide deck.

The only hand-offs left: the four methodology scores go to Member 1's comparison matrix in A2. The finished backlog sheet feeds Member 4's release roadmap in A3, done at the review call. The talk needs one full rehearsal.
