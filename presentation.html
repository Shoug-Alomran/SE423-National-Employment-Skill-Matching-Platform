Problem & Opportunity

Employment efforts are scattered — skills aren’t matched to jobs

The problem

National employment efforts are spread across different platforms, causing confusion. Manual procedures make finding suitable candidates hard, and current methods don’t accurately show whether a person’s skills fit the job.

The opportunity

A digital platform can reach people across the nation — including people with disabilities — and match them to jobs within their skill set, supporting the Labor Market Strategy and Human Capability Development Program.

7%

Unemployment target by 2030 (Vision 2030)

10.3%

Female unemployment in 2025, falling with participation

15%

Target employment of people with disabilities by 2030

Goals & Benefits

What Fursah sets out to do

One national platform

A unified employment and skill matching platform that refines how employers and applicants connect, replacing traditional job searching.

Skill-based matching

Propose opportunities from users’ skills and experience; help employers find applicants who meet their requirements.

Close skill gaps

Recommend learning programs based on qualifications and interests so users can spot and close gaps in their skills.

Faster, better hiring

Less time and effort to find matches, a stronger recruitment process, and better career opportunities.

Methodology Analysis

Four methodologies, two families

Scrum Agile

Daily communication, adaptable plans, rapid feedback and retrospectives

Focus on finishing work

Assumes ideal communicators; risk of burnout

Replacing members disrupts schedule

XP Agile

TDD and continuous integration catch defects early

Pair programming spreads knowledge

Demands strong technical discipline

Pairing clashes with matrix assignments

Waterfall Plan-driven

Documented requirements for PDPL and HRSD approval

Fixed cost and schedule; phase gates

Poor at handling change

Nothing usable until the end

Incremental Plan-driven

Core value early; matching engine risk tackled first

Ministry gate review per increment

Requirements locked early

Integration work grows each increment

Fit with Team, Organization & Stakeholders

Only the adaptive methods suit a creative, matrix team

Method Creative team Matrix organization Stakeholders
Scrum Direct match — built for iteration and change Built for quick response, like the matrix Sprint reviews and backlog input via the Product Owner
XP Supports experimenting with CV parsing and matching CI and shared code help; pairing is hard with competing work Frequent feedback, but needs timely participation
Waterfall Designs must be final before ideas are tested Partially — fixed phases aid planning but slow response Good for HRSD approvals; users give feedback only at the end
Incremental Better than Waterfall, less flexible than Agile Clear staffing per increment; change control slows response Predictable milestones; users shape later increments

Selected Process Methodology

We chose Scrum

New, evolving product

Fursah involves risk-taking, experimentation and iteration — Scrum is built for that.

No predictive methods

Requirements aren’t well defined at the start, ruling out Waterfall and Incremental.

Scrum over XP

Responds quickly to customer needs, makes change easy, and copes with an unstable scope.

Scrum Process

The meetings that keep each sprint on track

Meeting When Length Purpose
Sprint Planning Start of each sprint Up to 4 hours Set the sprint goal and select the stories
Daily Scrum Each working day 15 minutes Share progress, plan the day, raise problems
Sprint Review End of each sprint Up to 2 hours Show features to employers, job seekers and HRSD
Retrospective After the review Up to 1.5 hours Agree improvements for the next sprint
Backlog Refinement Once each sprint 1–2 hours Review, split and estimate upcoming stories

Also in the plan: Sprint 0 for preparation, epics to group stories, timeboxed research spikes (SP-01 to SP-03) and a shared Definition of Done.

Project Metrics

Four measures turn delivery into decisions

4.1 Schedule Progress

Velocity, burndown and release progress measured against the sprint and release plans.

4.2 Defect Rate / Quality

Defects by severity, defect density and open defects to protect trust and compliance.

4.3 Smart Matching Accuracy

Skill extraction, recommendation relevance and language fairness for Arabic and English.

4.4 Stakeholder Satisfaction

Feedback from sprint reviews, release acceptance and user surveys drives backlog priority.
